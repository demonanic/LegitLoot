# LegitLoot — Brand Art Direction

**Version:** 1.0  
**Approved:** 2026-11-07  
**Applies to:** Website, game-discovery experience, rewards UI, promotional artwork, mascot and future brand assets.  
**Status:** Approved working direction. Mascot exploration is complete enough for the platform build; do not spend further cycles on character development unless a concrete asset problem blocks implementation.

## 1. Brand idea

**LegitLoot: The Underground Arcade.**

LegitLoot is a rebellious, character-led gaming and rewards platform. It should feel like discovering a hidden arcade run by a crew of punk outsiders, hackers and obsessive gamers — not like entering a generic fintech dashboard, survey site or AI-generated gaming template.

The brand combines three attitudes:

- **Punk outlaw:** independent, irreverent, scrappy, confident and a little mischievous.
- **Underground hacker:** clever, resourceful, technically curious, gadget-minded and street-smart.
- **Arcade boss:** playful, competitive, theatrical, reward-driven and larger than life.

The result is a distinct hybrid. It is not grimdark, childish, corporate, sterile or a copy of a familiar cyberpunk franchise.

## 2. Core mascot

The badger is the primary brand character and the face of LegitLoot. The approved working personality is a nonchalant, money-motivated, game-obsessed hustler: lazy by default, opportunistic, sarcastic, gamble-minded and largely unbothered. He cares about making money, playing games and chasing the next score. His attitude is dry and funny rather than aggressive or heroic.

The existing concept is sufficient to support the website build. Treat the latest gritty comic-book concept as the visual direction; do not create additional mascot lore, elaborate character biographies, or repeated character-sheet variations as a separate workstream. Only produce the specific mascot poses or crops needed by the homepage and shared UI.

### Visual ingredients
- Strong, recognizable badger anatomy and facial markings.
- Punk hair / mohawk and expressive silhouette.
- Leather or patched streetwear with original LegitLoot insignia.
- Selective hacker details: custom goggles, improvised tech, cables, tools or wearable gadgets.
- Arcade details used as character props: dice, tokens, loot crates, game controllers or prize motifs.
- Expressive poses and facial acting: confident, amused, competitive, curious and triumphant.

### Consistency rules
- Preserve the same face shape, markings, proportions, signature hair, clothing language and key accessories across all art.
- Use the existing approved concept as the working reference. A full production character bible or additional turnaround sheet is not a prerequisite for finishing the platform.
- Do not casually change species, clothing, colors, age impression, anatomy or art style between pages.
- Do not substitute a generic animal mascot, emoji, stock illustration or improvised placeholder.
- The existing simple SVG badger is a temporary implementation placeholder, not approved final mascot artwork.

## 3. Visual treatment: deliberate mixed media

Use a controlled mixture of three complementary treatments. They must feel art-directed together, not randomly combined.

### A. Raw comic-book illustration
Use for mascot poses, action moments, featured game art and expressive promotional scenes. Strong silhouettes, confident linework, dynamic perspective, punchy contrast, hand-drawn detail and expressive character acting.

### B. Cinematic illustration
Use for hero scenes, environmental backgrounds and major featured-game panels. Use dramatic lighting, depth, atmospheric perspective and detailed environments. Keep the composition legible behind interface content.

### C. Distressed punk print
Use for labels, section dividers, badges, textures, posters and occasional framing elements. Use controlled halftone, ink wear, photocopy grain, screen-print imperfections, stencil shapes, torn-paper edges and graffiti marks.

**Mixing rule:** One treatment leads in each component; the other two support it. Do not apply every texture to every surface. Preserve clear text, readable controls and useful negative space.

## 4. Color and lighting

The core palette is drawn from the supplied LegitLoot reference:

- Midnight black / deep violet: primary environment and contrast.
- Electric purple: atmospheric depth, panel framing and secondary glow.
- Hot magenta: energy, highlights, borders and expressive accents.
- Radioactive lime: primary calls to action, key active states and high-priority reward cues.
- Electric cyan: technical details, secondary status and information cues.
- Warm gold: loot, prizes and special event accents.
- Off-white: primary text and icon contrast.

Use neon as intentional lighting, not as a blanket effect. Glow should emphasize focal points and interactive states. Avoid making every border, word and card glow equally.

## 5. Typography and graphic language

- Use bold, condensed display typography for major headings and poster-like statements.
- Use a highly legible sans-serif for interface copy, descriptions and forms.
- Use a restrained mono face for codes, stats, labels and technical microcopy.
- Use custom lettering or hand-treated display type sparingly for major brand moments.
- Keep small text readable on mobile; distressed effects must never compromise legibility.
- Prefer original iconography and a coherent icon family over arbitrary emoji or mixed icon sets.
- The LegitLoot wordmark and crown mark must be proper designed assets, not Unicode characters or emoji substitutions.

## 6. Interface personality and layout

The supplied dashboard mockup is the structural and visual reference, not a rigid pixel-perfect cage.

Preserve its recognizable design language:
- Strong branded header and navigation.
- Large illustrated hero with mascot, message and one dominant action.
- Player progression and reward-status strip.
- Distinct daily-loot, featured-game and earning-opportunity areas.
- Quests/challenges, event feature and leaderboard areas.
- Brand-principles / trust strip.
- Clear desktop navigation and compact mobile bottom navigation.

Improve the layout where necessary for responsive behavior, accessibility, content hierarchy and real product functionality. The mobile experience is a first-class design, not a squeezed desktop page.

Use asymmetry, layered composition, intentional overlap and varied panel proportions where they improve the experience. Avoid generic repeated rounded cards, excessive empty placeholder boxes, dashboard grids that all look identical, and template-like gradients.

Each major section should have its own visual focal point while remaining part of one recognizable brand system.

## 7. Artwork and asset standards

- Use the owner's supplied official game artwork for the actual games. Never invent screenshots or imply that generated art is an authentic game screenshot.
- Commission/generate original supporting scenes and mascot illustrations specifically for LegitLoot.
- Keep generated art free of baked-in UI text, buttons, reward amounts, logos and critical typography. Add those as real HTML text and components so they remain crisp, editable and responsive.
- Produce artwork at appropriate aspect ratios and resolutions for desktop, mobile and high-density screens.
- Maintain source assets and a simple asset manifest so each illustration has a clear purpose and location.
- Avoid repetitive AI visual tropes: random neon circuitry, anonymous hooded hackers, generic glowing city skylines, meaningless glyphs, overused purple gradients and generic robot/animal mascots.
- No stock art, placeholder SVG character or unrelated game image may be presented as final brand artwork.

## 8. Product truth and trust

Visual excitement must never imply functionality that is not live.

- Balances, XP, streaks, quests, leaderboard entries, reward amounts, timers and payout statuses must come from real connected data.
- If a feature is not implemented, label it clearly as preview, planned or inactive.
- Never fabricate user activity, social proof, earnings, partner approval, payout availability or scarcity.
- Preserve the distinction between game credits and cash rewards.
- Clear terms, status explanations and user control are part of the brand, not an afterthought.

## 9. Accessibility and responsive quality

- Maintain readable contrast, touch target sizes, visible focus states and semantic navigation.
- Support reduced motion and avoid distracting perpetual animation.
- Keep important text as real text, not embedded in images.
- Test at actual phone widths as well as desktop and tablet widths.
- The mobile dock must not cover content or form controls; account for safe-area insets.
- Use animation to communicate state or add character, never to obstruct a task.

## 10. Creative approval checklist

A proposed page or asset is not brand-approved until it passes these questions:

1. Does it feel like the LegitLoot Underground Arcade rather than a generic rewards dashboard?
2. Is the punk-outlaw / underground-hacker / arcade-boss personality recognizable?
3. Is the badger consistent with the approved working concept?
4. Does the mixed-media treatment feel intentional rather than randomly AI-generated?
5. Does the composition have a clear focal point and strong hierarchy?
6. Is the typography readable and the mobile layout considered?
7. Is actual game art used only for the game it represents?
8. Are all rewards, statuses and claims truthful?
9. Does the component work as a real interface, not merely as a picture?
10. Would the design still be recognizable as LegitLoot without the wordmark?

If the answer to any of these is no, revise before extending the design to more pages.

## 11. Workflow and ownership

- This document is the approved creative source of truth.
- The supplied dashboard mockup remains the primary visual reference.
- Do not continue mascot exploration unless a specific website asset requires it. Use the current concept as sufficient direction.
- Prioritize the complete responsive website framework and the supporting artwork needed by actual pages: hero/environment art, section backgrounds, game presentation assets, loot/reward motifs, and a coherent logo/icon set.
- Develop a reusable design system from the approved art direction, then apply it consistently to Home, Games, Earn, Deals, Rewards and Wallet.
- Keep implementation on the isolated development branch until the owner approves the actual device preview.
- Do not merge to production or change live reward, authentication or wallet behavior as part of a visual-only task.
