# LegitLoot Creative Bible

**Status:** Current owner-approved creative source of truth  
**Applies to:** LegitLoot brand expression, Skuz, public website, promotional art, and future original visual assets  
**Authority:** Current owner direction; this document supersedes conflicting visual and character guidance in older project material. Business, financial, privacy, security, and release rules remain governed by the Constitution and Platform Build Specification.

## Brand identity

LegitLoot is an underground organization operating a chaotic digital arcade. It is not a generic rewards website, corporate gaming platform, or manufactured punk brand. It should feel built and inhabited by a strange, resourceful crew, while the actual service remains honest and welcoming to players.

The fictional citizens are antiheroes and misfits: intimidating, sarcastic, brash, imperfect, charismatic, and ultimately considerate. They behave like a dysfunctional family. Loyalty and affection are shown through actions, not sentimental speeches.

Punk philosophy is the underlying DNA:

- DIY independence and creative freedom.
- Individuality and respect for difference.
- Skepticism toward corporate manipulation.
- Community built through mutual help and earned trust.

The website must remain welcoming, honest, accessible, and respectful toward users. Fictional characters may be abrasive with each other; the product must not demean or manipulate its audience.

## Artistic direction

- Mature western comic-book illustration.
- Heavy ink, expressive anatomy, confident silhouettes, crosshatching, and painterly shadows.
- Gritty underground locations, improvised machinery, lived-in clutter, and dystopian atmosphere.
- Original characters and original artwork created specifically for LegitLoot.
- No anime aesthetic.
- Do not imitate or reproduce existing copyrighted comic characters, panels, costumes, logos, or signature compositions.
- Punk influence must feel authentic through DIY material, individuality, wear, and attitude. Avoid decorative safety pins, generic mohawks, random slogans, and costume-like punk signals without context.
- Use visual storytelling, framing, gesture, props, and silence. Do not force slogans, punchlines, profanity, or marketing-style dialogue. Humor should arise naturally from characters and situations.

## Color identity

The core palette is near-black, charcoal, blood red, acid green, bone white, and industrial grey. Electric magenta and cyan are optional arcade accents. Use role-based semantic tokens so accents remain deliberate and text remains legible. Dark atmosphere must not reduce contrast or make the interface hostile to use.

Suggested initial token anchors, subject to contrast review in the implemented UI:

| Role | Starting value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Near-black | `#101110` | Main page environment |
| Charcoal | `#202221` | Primary panels and raised surfaces |
| Industrial grey | `#686b67` | Secondary rules, borders, quiet metadata |
| Bone white | `#f1eee3` | Main text and light graphic fields |
| Blood red | `#a9252b` | Strong emphasis, selected state, important markers |
| Acid green | `#c7ee3a` | Primary action and clear positive state |
| Electric magenta | `#e83f9d` | Limited arcade light accent |
| Electric cyan | `#45c6d5` | Limited technical/arcade accent |

The values are implementation starting points, not permission to use color without semantic meaning. Validate contrast for every text/control pairing.

## Personality and dialogue

Balance dark humor, absurd situational comedy, deadpan delivery, irreverence, and genuine humanity. Keep jokes occasional and character-driven. Silence and visual storytelling are equally valid. Avoid canned quips, relentless snark, forced catchphrases, gratuitous profanity, promotional patter, and text that competes with the task a player came to complete.

The platform voice is candid and plain-spoken. State what is available, planned, disabled, or unknown. Never use character banter to obscure terms, earning conditions, financial status, privacy information, or support guidance.

## Skuz — character continuity

Skuz is the embodiment and spokesperson of LegitLoot. He is a reluctant caretaker and tour guide: nobody else seems qualified, interested, or aware that things need doing. He complains, offers occasional unsolicited advice, helps when necessary, and pretends to care less than he actually does.

The supplied Skuz reference is the character foundation. Preserve its recognizable badger markings and facial structure, spiked mohawk, green eyes, cigar, spiked leather jacket, punk personality, and distinctive expression. Do not casually alter his species, face, silhouette, signature clothing, or defining accessories. Do not turn him into a generic advertising mascot or make him constantly deliver catchphrases.

Create and maintain a consistent visual reference sheet and expression library. The reference should establish clear views, proportions, facial markings, mohawk silhouette, eyes, cigar, jacket, spikes, and a small useful range of readable expressions. New poses should derive from that reference; expression changes must not become character redesigns. The reference sheet is a continuity tool, not a public-facing UI asset.

If an image-generation tool is used, label the supplied image strictly as the Skuz identity reference. Request original artwork in the specified western comic medium. Do not request imitation of a named artist, franchise, or existing comic.

## Website expression

The website is an actual product interface wearing the world's art direction, not a flattened comic panel and not an in-character sales pitch. Establish a clear hierarchy between navigation, games, opportunities, account status, and support. Keep forms, reward explanations, legal content, financial status, and accessibility controls calm and direct.

Use asymmetry, inked dividers, hand-made texture, expressive image crops, and varied panel shapes in editorial areas. Keep dense content and transactional layouts steady and easy to scan. Motion should be brief, optional, and reduced-motion aware; do not use flashing, forced parallax, or perpetual movement.

## Non-negotiable product truth

- Use only the approved initial games: Demonanic, Gauntlet's Survival, and Harvest//Zero.
- Use official game art only for its actual game. Never present generated artwork as gameplay or a screenshot.
- Do not invent users, balances, transactions, earnings, reward rates, partner approvals, availability, payout claims, or scarcity.
- Keep LootCoins/game credits separate from any future USD cash ledger. Game credits have no cash value, are non-transferable, and cannot be withdrawn or converted.
- Keep disabled, planned, and preview systems clearly identified. Fiction cannot imply a real feature is live.
- Keep important copy, labels, forms, navigation, amounts, statuses, and controls in HTML, never baked into generated art.

## Approval checklist

1. Does the work feel like a lived-in underground organization and chaotic digital arcade?
2. Is the art original mature western comic illustration, with no anime aesthetic or copyrighted imitation?
3. Are heavy ink, crosshatching, expressive anatomy, and painterly shadow used with control?
4. Does punk material feel lived-in and authentic rather than decorative or performative?
5. Is Skuz unmistakably consistent with the supplied reference?
6. Does humor arise naturally, with room for silence and genuine care?
7. Is the real interface welcoming, truthful, accessible, and understandable without character dialogue?
8. Are all game, reward, provider, and financial claims verified and accurately labeled?
9. Is the page's focal point clear and its text easy to read on a phone?
10. Does the design respect reduced motion, keyboard use, touch use, and safe areas?

Any failure involving character continuity, product truth, contrast, or usability blocks acceptance.


## Owner visual direction update — 2026-11-11

The owner selected the uploaded Skuz portrait as the definitive visual target for the website's comic-book energy. This direction supersedes any interpretation that makes the brand uniformly bleak, monochrome, or post-apocalyptic.

- Match the supplied reference's bold, high-energy western comic illustration: expressive character proportions, heavy clean ink contours, halftone texture, punchy highlights, and dynamic graphic framing.
- Keep Skuz's reference identity intact. Do not exaggerate his animal anatomy beyond the supplied design or redesign his face, mohawk, green eyes, cigar, jacket, or silhouette.
- The atmosphere is a lively underground arcade, not a dead wasteland. Use active arcade cabinets, colorful practical lighting, improvised details, graffiti-like crown motifs, and a sense of mischief and movement.
- Use vivid acid green as the strongest accent, with magenta, cyan, and occasional orange for arcade energy. Keep near-black as a grounding color, not as an excuse to make every scene dark.
- Reserve dystopian bleakness for a specific game world such as Harvest//Zero; do not impose it on the LegitLoot brand environment or every game.
- Website sections may use comic-panel framing, angled borders, halftone fields, and vivid color-coded game art. Critical interface text, controls, status and product terms remain real HTML and accessible.
- Use the uploaded reference as the visual authority when creating new Skuz artwork. Generated concept images containing baked-in interface text or unsupported product claims are inspiration only and must not be used as website assets.
